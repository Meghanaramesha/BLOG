Lately, I keep seeing AI discussed as something you “add” to a team.  
  
The intention is usually good. But AI doesn’t live in isolation.

![](https://www.gamigion.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2-Ways-AI-Shows-Up-in-Game-Production.jpg)

#### When it’s treated like a plugin:

* Artists feel unsure or threatened
* Cost savings are expected, but never clearly defined
* No one really owns the workflow

---

#### What actually works in practice is much less exciting:

* Deciding where AI belongs in the pipeline
* Educating artists before expecting results
* Building templates and blueprints instead of one-off usage
* Focusing on reuse, not just generation

*That’s when AI becomes boring and useful.*And in production, boring is usually a good sign!

![](https://www.gamigion.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/MCHL-100x100.jpeg)
