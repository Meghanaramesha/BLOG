𝐈 𝐬𝐩𝐞𝐧𝐭 𝐰𝐞𝐞𝐤𝐬 𝐛𝐥𝐚𝐦𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐜𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐯𝐞𝐬 𝐟𝐨𝐫 𝐢𝐧𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐬𝐢𝐬𝐭𝐞𝐧𝐭 𝐑𝐎𝐀𝐒. 𝐓𝐮𝐫𝐧𝐬 𝐨𝐮𝐭 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐚𝐥𝐠𝐨𝐫𝐢𝐭𝐡𝐦 𝐜𝐨𝐮𝐥𝐝 𝐧𝐨𝐭 𝐬𝐞𝐞 𝐞𝐧𝐨𝐮𝐠𝐡.

We ran 72 days of Android campaigns.  
Daily spend averaged $284. This is a soft launch app that wants to prove itself.  
D0 ROAS swung from 2% to 40% with no clear pattern.  
  
So I did what any data nerd would do. I made a scatter plot.  
  
I skipped spend vs ROAS. I plotted Paying Users vs D0 ROAS.  
  
Correlation: 0.51  
  
Now we are getting somewhere.  
  
The pattern:  
  
• 0 to 2 payers: 3% average D0 ROAS  
• 3 to 4 payers: 8% average D0 ROAS  
• 5 to 6 payers: 16% average D0 ROAS  
• 7+ payers: 20% average D0 ROAS  
  
That is a 7x jump from low signal days to high signal days.  
  
Why this matters:  
  
Meta optimizes for conversions. It learns from events, not dollars.  
  
Spending $500 with 2 payers gives the algorithm the same signal as spending $200 with 2 payers.  
  
When I checked daily spend vs D0 ROAS, the correlation was 0.20.  
Basically noise. So don’t dilute the budget in different campaigns.  
  
Payers vs ROAS was 0.51.  
That is a real relationship.  
  
The problem we found:  
  
51% of our active days had fewer than 5 paying users. That is where performance got unstable.  
  
Split view:  
• Below 5 payers: 5% average D0 ROAS  
• 5+ payers: 18% average D0 ROAS  
  
That is 3.5x better once we cleared the signal threshold.  
  
Creatives still matter. We saw jumps when we switched concepts.  
App updates also change conversion, onboarding, and monetization.  
  
But here is what I missed: even great creatives underperform when the algorithm does not get enough signal to guide delivery.  
  
What this changed for us:  
  
We stopped asking, “How much should we spend?”  
We started asking, “How do we get more payers per day into one campaign?”  
  
The answer was smarter spend.  
Consolidating budget so the algorithm gets clean, concentrated signal instead of scattered noise across multiple campaigns.  
  
If your ROAS is inconsistent and you have already tested creatives, audiences, and bid strategies, look at your daily payer count.  
It might be the one you are overlooking.  
  
67 active days. 374 total payers. One scatter plot that added a new lens.
